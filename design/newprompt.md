01
What GoldOak is
Your original write-up had the right instinct and buried it in the middle. The instinct is this: the client comes first, not the insurer. Everything below is that sentence turned into a structure.

The definition
GoldOak is an insurance solutions agency serving corporate, SME and individual clients. It identifies a client's risk exposure, designs an insurance programme around that exposure, sources and compares options from a panel of insurers, implements the chosen solution, and manages the relationship through service, claims and review.

We do not sell policies. We solve exposures, and a policy is what the solution is made of.
Four operating principles
These are the rules that settle arguments. When a decision is unclear, one of these decides it.

Risk before product
No recommendation is made before the exposure is understood and written down. A quotation request that arrives without a risk picture is incomplete work.

Appropriate over cheap
The objective is the most suitable cover for the client's circumstances. Price is one variable among cover, limits, exclusions, excess, and the insurer's ability to pay and to service.

Options, not assumptions
Where the market allows, the client sees more than one option and sees them compared on the same terms. We never assume which insurer wins.

The relationship outlives the policy
Issuing the policy is the middle of the job, not the end. Service, claims and review are the product.

What GoldOak is not
Not a quotation service. Anyone can forward a rate.
Not a single-insurer distribution arm. The panel exists to give the client choice.
Not transactional. A client who only hears from us at renewal is a client we are about to lose.
One structural question to settle first
The document you wrote describes broker behaviour — representing the client's interest, going to multiple insurers, comparing the market — while using the word agency. In Kenya those are two different regulated things: an agent is appointed by and acts for insurers, a broker acts for the client, and the licensing, capital, professional indemnity and disclosure requirements differ. There are also multi-agency arrangements that allow an agent to represent several insurers.

This is not a wording problem. It determines what you can legally promise a client, how you are remunerated, and what you can put on a proposal document. Before any of the structure below is printed or published, confirm with the IRA and your compliance advisor which category GoldOak holds today and which one this model actually requires. I'm not giving you a legal opinion — I'm flagging that the model and the licence have to agree.

Everything else in this document works under either answer. Only the language changes.

02
Client segments
Three segments. The point of defining them is not tidiness — it is so that anyone in the agency can place a new enquiry within a minute and know what happens next. Segment on complexity of exposure first and size second.

Segment	Placed here when	Service model	Contact rhythm
Corporate	Formal procurement, board or HR sign-off, three or more classes of cover, or premium above a threshold you set	Named account manager, written insurance programme, tender and RFQ handling, structured annual review	Quarterly contact minimum, formal review before renewal
SME	Owner or a single manager decides, business has staff, premises, stock, vehicles or client contracts	Whole-business risk review, staged build-up of cover, plain-language recommendations	Twice yearly contact, review at renewal
Individual	Personal or family exposure only	Needs conversation, two or more options, guided choice, personal onboarding	Annual review, contact on life events
Why SME is the engine
A corporate account is valuable but slow to win and heavily contested. An individual is fast to win and worth little on its own. An SME is the only segment where one relationship, won once, routinely carries six or seven exposures: the business itself, its employees, its premises, its equipment, its vehicles, its customers, and the owner's own family.

So measure SME work by a different number. Not policies sold — covers per client relationship. If that number is drifting toward one, the agency has quietly become a policy shop again.

The SME conversation, in order
What does the business actually do, and where does its money come from?
What does it own, and what would it cost to replace?
Who works here, and what happens to them if something goes wrong?
Who could sue it, and for what?
What would stop it trading for a month?
What is insured today, and by whom, and when does it renew?
Six questions. Answered honestly, they produce a risk register that no competitor forwarding a rate can match.

04
The insurer panel
The panel is an asset, not a contact list. Managed properly it is the reason a client uses GoldOak instead of going direct.

Current panel: Jubilee, APA, AAR, AIG, Britam, CIC, Old Mutual, Prudential.

Three things to do to the panel
Classify it
Map each insurer against the solutions catalogue in section 03 — which classes they are licensed and willing to write, not which classes they have a brochure for. The output is a grid: solutions down the side, insurers across the top. That grid immediately shows you where the panel is thin and where you have no one to go to at all.

Tier it
Not every insurer is used the same way. Sort them into: primary — go to first for this class; secondary — used for comparison and for risks the primary declines; specialist — used only where they have a genuine edge. Tiering stops placement being a matter of who answered the phone.

Document it
One factsheet per insurer, kept current, structured the same way every time:

Identity
Licence class, financial standing, group ownership, our agency or placement agreement and its terms

Appetite
Classes written, target market, risks they want, risks they decline, minimum and maximum sizes

Commercial
Commission structure, discretion available, premium payment terms, credit arrangements

Operations
Underwriting requirements and documents, quotation turnaround, portal or system access, policy issuance time, named contacts with mobile numbers

Claims
Notification route, documents required, assessor arrangements, average settlement time, our own recorded experience of them

Conditions
Exclusions and warranties that catch clients out and must be explained at onboarding

Our record
Business placed, loss ratio, service incidents, review date and standing on the panel

The skill this builds is not "Jubilee has product X." It is "this client has this exposure — who on our panel can carry it, and which of them will actually pay the claim quickly."
Placement rule
Set a minimum and hold to it, so that "we compared the market" is a fact rather than a claim:

Individual — at least two options, unless the client instructs otherwise in writing.
SME — at least three options on any class above a value you set.
Corporate — three to five, and a written comparison schedule every time.
Review the panel formally once a year: who to keep, who to demote, who to add, and where the gaps are.

05
The GoldOak process
This was the strongest part of your draft. The improvement it needs is that every stage must produce something that exists — a document, a record, a file. A stage with no output cannot be trained, delegated, checked, or improved. Six stages, six outputs.

Understand
Build the risk picture before anything else. Who is the client, what do they own, what do they do, who depends on them, what could go wrong, what would it cost them, what is insured now, and what is missing.

OUTPUT — Client risk profile. A completed fact-find with a written list of identified exposures and current cover. Nothing proceeds without it.

Solve
Design the programme around the exposures found. Which risks must be transferred, which can be retained, which should be reduced first. Priorities where budget is limited, and a sequence for what comes later.

OUTPUT — Risk and recommendation summary. Exposure, our recommendation, why, and what happens if it is left uninsured.

Compare
Approach the appropriate panel insurers and put the responses side by side on identical terms: cover, limits, benefits, exclusions, excess, conditions, premium, and the insurer's service and claims record.

OUTPUT — Comparison schedule, plus a stated recommendation. Presenting three quotes without a view is passing the work back to the client.

Implement
Proposal forms, supporting documents, underwriting requirements, premium, cover confirmation, policy issuance, delivery of documents, and a walk-through of the conditions and warranties that could void a claim.

OUTPUT — Onboarding pack and a completed client file: policy documents, schedule, a plain-language summary of what is and is not covered, claims instructions, and contacts.

Support
The live relationship. Questions, endorsements, additions and deletions, member changes, certificates, premium reminders, and claims. Contact on the rhythm set for the segment — proactively, not only when the client calls.

OUTPUT — Service log against the client record. Every interaction dated and recorded, so the file survives any one person leaving.

Review
Broader than renewal, because exposures change first. New staff, vehicles, premises, contracts, equipment, revenue, borrowing, directors. The question is whether the programme still fits the business as it is now — asked on a schedule, not only when the policy is about to expire.

OUTPUT — Review report and renewal recommendation, issued at least 45 days before expiry so there is time to remarket properly.

Read the stages as a loop, not a line. Review feeds straight back into Understand. A client who has been through two cycles should have a risk profile that is visibly richer than the one built on day one. If it looks the same, the review was a renewal notice wearing a different name.

06
Claims: the moment of truth
Claims sits inside Support, but it deserves its own defined procedure because it is the only moment where the client finds out whether the last three years were worth paying for. It is also where referrals are made or lost.

Step	What happens	Standard
Notify	Client reaches a named person, not a general line. Immediate acknowledgement and first guidance on what to do and what not to do.	Same day
Register	Claim logged internally and reported to the insurer. Reference obtained and given to the client.	Within 24 hours
Document	We tell the client exactly what is needed and help assemble it. We do not forward a requirements list and wait.	Within 48 hours
Track	We chase the insurer and the assessor, and update the client on a fixed rhythm whether or not there is news.	Weekly update
Settle	Offer reviewed before it reaches the client. Where it is short or wrongly declined, we argue the point.	Reviewed within 48 hours
Close	Confirm settlement, reinstate cover where needed, record the outcome against the insurer's panel factsheet.	Within 7 days
That last step matters more than it looks. Every claim teaches you something about an insurer that no brochure will. Recorded consistently, it becomes the evidence behind your placement decisions.

08
The operating spine
Structure without an operating spine is a diagram. These six things are what make the structure run, and they are the gap between where the write-up ended and where a working agency starts.

1. One client record
A single place holding every client: contacts, risk profile, all policies across all insurers, renewal dates, premium status, claims history, and every interaction. The renewal diary is generated from it, not kept separately in someone's head. Start in a spreadsheet if you must, but design it as the eventual database — one row per client, one row per policy, linked.

2. Templates for every stage output
Six stages, six documents. Fact-find, risk and recommendation summary, comparison schedule, onboarding pack, service log, review report. Once these exist and carry the GoldOak name, the quality of the work stops depending on who did it.

3. Service standards, written down
Trigger	Response
New enquiry	Acknowledged same day, fact-find booked within 2 working days
Quotation request to insurers	Options back to client within 5 working days
Client instruction to proceed	Cover confirmed within 2 working days
Policy documents	Delivered within 14 days of issuance
General query	Answered within 24 hours
Renewal	Review report 45 days before expiry
Adjust the numbers to what you can actually hold. A standard you miss is worse than no standard.

4. Roles and a training pathway
Even at small scale, name who owns what: business development, servicing and administration, claims, and compliance. One person may hold three of them today — that is fine, as long as it is written, because it is the map for who you hire first.

For Terry and anyone who follows her, the training path runs in this order: the process → the solutions catalogue → the panel. Learn the six stages, then learn what each class of cover does and who needs it, then learn which insurers write it. Learning the panel first produces someone who can quote. Learning it last produces someone who can advise.

5. Compliance register
Licence and renewal dates, professional indemnity, agency agreements with each insurer, premium handling arrangements, and data protection — you hold medical, financial and identity data on every client, and the Data Protection Act obligations that come with that are real and worth confirming with your advisor.

6. The numbers you watch
Retention rate at renewal, by segment
Covers per client relationship, especially in SME
New clients by source — referral, direct, introducer
Revenue by class and by insurer, so you see concentration before it becomes a risk
Claims settled, and average days from notification to settlement
Pipeline: enquiries in, quoted, converted

Sequencing the build
You asked to lay the foundation first, so the growth plan is deliberately sketched here rather than detailed. It comes next, and it will be much easier to write once the answers in section 10 exist.

Months 0–6
Foundation
Settle the regulatory question. Build the client record and load every existing client into it. Write the six templates. Complete the panel grid and the insurer factsheets. Set service standards. Put five years of existing business onto a renewal diary — that alone usually finds revenue you already had.

Months 6–18
Depth
Run the full process on every new client and retro-fit it to existing ones at their next renewal. Build the solution one-pagers. Train against them. Pick one SME niche you understand well and go deep rather than broad. Start measuring covers per client.

Months 18+
Scale
Hire against the roles already named. Add panel capacity where the grid shows gaps. Move from spreadsheet to a proper agency system. Pursue corporate accounts on the strength of a documented process and a claims record you can show.
